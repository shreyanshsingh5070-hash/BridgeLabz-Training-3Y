The Craft of Readable Text

Good typography is invisible when it works, and jarring when it doesn't. This short article walks through a few of the CSS properties that shape how comfortable text is to read.

Font choice sets the tone before a single word is read. Pairing a bold serif heading with a calmer sans-serif body creates contrast without noise. Italic emphasis, used sparingly, draws the eye to a single idea rather than shouting across a whole sentence. You can also highlight a key phrase the way a reader might with a highlighter pen.

Line height and letter spacing matter just as much as the typeface itself. Text set too tightly feels cramped and tiring to read, while text spaced too loosely starts to fall apart into individual words rather than reading as flowing sentences. A comfortable middle ground keeps the eye moving smoothly from line to line.
